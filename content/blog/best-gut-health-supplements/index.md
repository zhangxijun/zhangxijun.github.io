Probiotic labels compete on one number, and the numbers barely relate to each other. Physician's
Choice prints 60 billion CFU and costs about 83 cents a day. Align prints 1 billion and costs 96
cents. The cheaper bottle also carries 146,339 Amazon ratings against Align's 1,024.

That gap explains the whole shelf. Align sells one strain, Bifidobacterium longum 35624, which is
the strain most IBS trials have used. Physician's Choice sells ten strains plus a prebiotic at a
lower cost per billion. Neither bottle is better in the abstract; they answer different questions,
and the label does not tell you which one you are buying. Seven of the ten picks below are
probiotics; one is a digestive enzyme and two are fiber.

## Why take a gut supplement at all

Probiotics are not a general wellness upgrade. The strongest evidence sits in narrow cases:
antibiotic-associated diarrhea, some IBS symptoms, and preventing Clostridioides difficile diarrhea
in people taking antibiotics. A 2020 review in the journal Gut found strain-specific effects, which
is why "10 strains" is not automatically better than one strain with a trial behind it.

Fiber has a plainer case. Most adults eat around 15 grams a day against a recommendation of roughly
25 to 38 grams, and soluble fiber from psyllium or wheat dextrin helps with regularity at a fraction
of the price of a probiotic. If constipation is the main complaint, fiber is usually the cheaper
first move. Enzymes are narrower still: they help break down fat, protein and carbohydrate in a
meal, so they suit people who feel heavy after fatty food, not people chasing a microbiome fix.

What none of these do is repair a damaged gut lining in a week, replace a diagnosis, or cancel a
highly processed diet. They are small tools with specific jobs, and the cost per serving decides
whether a three-month trial is worth running at all. At 37 cents a day, a three-month trial costs
about \$34. At 96 cents, it costs about \$88.

## What the main gut ingredients actually do

| Ingredient | What it does | What to check on the label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CFU | The count of live cultures in the bottle | CFU at the end of shelf life, not at manufacture |
| Strain | Different strains do different jobs | Genus, species and strain code (LGG, 35624) |
| Prebiotic | Feeds the cultures already in your gut | Grams of inulin, FOS or wheat dextrin |
| Digestive enzymes | Breaks down fat, protein and carbs in a meal | Units per capsule and which enzymes are listed |
| Psyllium | Soluble fiber that bulks and softens stool | Grams per serving and the liquid you must drink |
| Wheat dextrin | Soluble fiber that dissolves clear | Grams per serving and whether it is flavored |
| Delayed-release capsule | Helps cultures survive stomach acid | "Delayed release" or "enteric coated" on the bottle |
| Shelf-stable format | No refrigeration needed | Storage instructions and expiry count |

Two label habits cause most of the confusion. First, CFU counts are measured at different points.
A bottle that says "1 billion CFU" may be promising that at the expiry date, while a "60 billion"
bottle may be counting at manufacture, before the cultures die off in storage. Second, strain
matters more than species. "Lactobacillus acidophilus" covers many strains with different research
behind them; a strain code is the only way to match a bottle to a study.

Fiber labels have their own trap. Benefiber's serving is two teaspoons for 3 grams of fiber, so a
heaped spoon quietly changes the dose. Metamucil's serving is 2.4 grams of soluble fiber and
requires a full glass of water; taking it dry is uncomfortable and, in rare cases, risky.

## How we picked

- **Amazon category rankings, then the label** — probiotics were checked against Amazon's
  Acidophilus and probiotic categories, enzymes against multi-enzyme, fiber against dietary fiber.
  Rank tells you what is selling; the Supplement Facts panel tells you what you are actually
  swallowing, and the two often disagree.
- **Cost per serving, not per bottle** — a \$24.97 bottle of 30 capsules costs more per day than a
  \$21.95 bottle of 60. CFU per dollar matters as much as CFU per capsule, which is why a 1-billion
  product can be the most expensive pick here.
- **Strain and format before strain count** — one strain with trials behind it beats ten strains
  with no research, and a delayed-release capsule or a blister pack usually signals a more careful
  formula. Storage instructions decided two picks outright.
- **Reviews with the complaints left in** — quotes come from iHerb verified purchases, copied as
  written. Gas, bloating, a 4.4-star average and a one-star rejection all stayed in.

## FAQ

**Do probiotics need to be refrigerated?**
Only if the label says so. Culturelle, Align, Renew Life, Jarrow and Physician's Choice are
shelf-stable; Garden of Life's bottle prefers 22°C or cooler. A shelf-stable bottle left in a hot car
still loses viability, so treat "no refrigeration" as a label claim rather than permission to bake it.

**Is 60 billion CFU better than 10 billion?**
Not automatically, and the count does not say which strains you are getting. Culturelle's 10 billion
is one studied strain (LGG); Physician's Choice's 60 billion is split across ten strains. Match the
strain to your problem, then compare cost per serving within that group.

**Can I take a probiotic and a digestive enzyme together?**
Yes, they do different jobs. Enzymes act on the meal in your stomach; probiotics are live cultures
that settle in the gut over weeks. If you are adding both, start the enzyme at one meal a day so you
can tell which one is causing gas or loose stools.

**How long before I notice anything?**
Give it four to six weeks at the labeled dose, which is why the cost per serving matters: a
60-serving bottle gives you a real trial, a 16-serving bottle does not. Stop and talk to a clinician
if symptoms worsen, and do not use probiotics to self-treat a persistent change in bowel habits.

## A safety note

Probiotics are low risk for healthy adults, but they are not risk-free for everyone. People with a
weakened immune system, a central line, short bowel syndrome or a critical illness should not start
a probiotic without a doctor's approval, because rare bloodstream infections have been reported.
Stop if you develop fever, severe abdominal pain or bloody diarrhea.

Psyllium and wheat dextrin need water and a slow ramp; starting at three servings a day causes
bloating. Anyone with a swallowing disorder should avoid psyllium powder, and fiber can interfere
with how some medications are absorbed, so space them by two hours. Digestive enzymes can affect
blood sugar in people with diabetes, and anyone with pancreatitis or a digestive disease should ask
a clinician before using them.

These products do not diagnose or treat IBS, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis or an infection.
If your bowel habits change for more than two weeks, or you see blood, get medical advice rather
than another bottle. This article is general information, not medical advice.
