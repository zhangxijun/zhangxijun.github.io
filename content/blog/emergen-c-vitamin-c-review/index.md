Emergen-C is the orange packet that shows up in office drawers, gym bags and airport carry-ons
every winter. The pitch is simple: tear one open, add water, and you have 1,000 mg of vitamin C
plus a handful of B vitamins and electrolytes. No pill to swallow, no bottle to count.

The Super Orange version is the original and still the best seller — 4.7 stars across roughly 2,900 reviews on iHerb as of Nov 14, 2026, at **\$15.43 for 30 packets** — which works out to **\$0.51 a serving**.

Is it worth eight times the price of a plain vitamin C capsule? It depends on what you are
actually buying it for. Here is the honest breakdown.

## Quick verdict

- **Best for:** travel, people who hate swallowing pills, and anyone who wants vitamin C plus
  B vitamins and electrolytes in one step.
- **Skip if:** you are watching added sugar (6 g per packet), you want the cheapest possible
  vitamin C (a capsule costs about 6 cents), or you are buying for a child under 14.
- **What stands out:** a full 1,000 mg of vitamin C per packet, with real label extras — zinc,
  magnesium, potassium and seven B vitamins — that most cheap vitamin C products do not include.

## What you actually get

Every fact below comes from the packet label (serving size 9.1 g), not from the marketing copy:

- **Vitamin C 1,000 mg** (1,111% of the daily value), as ascorbic acid and zinc ascorbate
- **B vitamins:** B1 0.36 mg, B2 0.39 mg, B3 4 mg, B6 10 mg, B12 25 mcg, pantothenic acid 2.5 mg,
  folate 167 mcg DFE
- **Minerals:** zinc 2 mg, manganese 0.5 mg, magnesium 53 mg, potassium 200 mg, calcium 50 mg
- **Electrolytes:** the label flags magnesium and potassium with an electrolyte symbol
- **Sugar:** 6 g per packet (6 g added sugar, 12% of the daily value), 8 g total carbohydrate,
  35 calories
- **Sodium:** 65 mg per packet
- **Sweetened with:** fructose. No artificial sweeteners; the box is caffeine-free, gluten-free
  and vegetarian

three that carry the immune-support pitch; the B vitamins are about energy metabolism, and the
minerals double as electrolytes.

Two things worth noticing. First, the B vitamins are uneven: B1, B2 and niacin sit at about
25-30% of the daily value, pantothenic acid and folate land around 40-50%, while B6 (10 mg) and
B12 (25 mcg) run far past 100%. Second, the magnesium (53 mg) and potassium (200 mg) are the
reason the box calls itself an electrolyte drink — the amounts are modest, but they are listed
on the panel rather than hidden in a blend.

## What I like

**1. One packet covers the whole day.** The daily value for vitamin C is 90 mg for adults, so
1,000 mg is more than ten times that. You are not topping up — you are taking a full dose in one
step, which is what most people actually want from a vitamin C product.

**2. It is not just vitamin C.** The B vitamins, zinc, magnesium and potassium are listed right on
the panel, not buried in a proprietary blend. A $6 bottle of ascorbic acid gives you none of that,
so part of the price difference is real.

**3. No pill.** Dissolve one packet in 4-6 ounces of water and drink it. For anyone who gags on
large tablets or already takes four capsules in the morning, this is the whole point.

**4. Travel-proof packaging.** Individual foil packets survive a gym bag, a glove box or a carry-on
without a pill organizer, and you never have to count how many are left in a bottle.

**5. Predictable taste and mixability.** It is sweet, fizzy and orange — closer to a soft drink
than a supplement. If you like that, you will actually finish the box, which is more than most
supplements can claim.

## Where it falls short

**The sugar is real.** 6 g per packet is about a teaspoon and a half, and it is all added sugar.
One packet a day is fine for most people, but if you are watching your intake, a capsule has zero.

**It is expensive per dose.** \$0.51 a packet against roughly \$0.06 for a 1,000 mg capsule is a big
gap. You are paying for flavor, convenience and the extra B vitamins, not for the vitamin C itself.

**The taste is not for everyone.** Sweet, fizzy and slightly salty from the 65 mg of sodium. People
who expect a neutral supplement often find it too much like candy.

**Foil packets are waste.** Thirty sachets per box, none of them recyclable in most curbside
programs. A bottle is the greener choice if you take vitamin C daily at home.

## How to use it

The label directions are short: **ages 14 and up, one packet daily**. Empty it into a glass, add
4-6 ounces of water and stir. More water means a lighter flavor. Do not exceed the suggested use,
and store the box at room temperature away from moisture.

## Who should skip it

Skip it if you are pregnant or nursing without asking your doctor first, if you take prescription
medication, or if you are buying for a child under 14 — the label is written for adults and teens
14 and older. If you are managing blood sugar, the 6 g of added sugar per packet is worth counting
before you make it a daily habit.

## FAQ

**Is 1,000 mg of vitamin C too much?** For healthy adults, the tolerable upper intake level is
2,000 mg a day, so one packet stays inside the limit. Going well above that in a day can cause
digestive upset rather than extra benefit.

**Can I take it on an empty stomach?** The label does not restrict timing. Some people prefer it
with food because the powder is acidic; try one packet and see how you feel.

**Does it replace a multivitamin?** No. It covers vitamin C generously and seven B vitamins at
uneven levels, but it has no vitamin A, D, E or K — it is not a full multivitamin.

## Bottom line

Emergen-C Super Orange does exactly what it promises: 1,000 mg of vitamin C, plus B vitamins,
zinc and electrolytes, in a packet you can drink anywhere. It is sweet, it costs about $0.51 a
serving, and it is a poor value if all you want is ascorbic acid. For travel, for pill-avoiders
and for anyone who will actually finish the box, it earns its place — and 4 out of 5 editor
stars, with the missing star going to the added sugar and the price per dose.
