21st Century started in 1991 in Tempe, Arizona, building its brand on one promise: the lowest per-serving cost for basic supplements. A 300-tablet bottle of Sentry multivitamin costs $16.99 and delivers 300 days, which at 6 cents a serving is the lowest price point we have measured for any adult multivitamin on either iHerb or Amazon. The company was acquired by Grant Avenue Capital in January 2026, but the catalog and pricing model have not changed.

The brand targets shoppers who need volume and consistency. It does not pursue third-party certifications beyond basic formula declarations, and most products use generic forms rather than patented ingredients. That choice is intentional — swapping zinc citrate for zinc glycinate would raise the price by 30–40%, and 21st Century's buyer is the one who wants zinc at 5 cents a serving, not 7 cents.

## 1. What the brand actually is

21st Century's identity rests on three things.

**Ultra-low per-serving cost.** The economics are straightforward: large bottles, simple formulas, and manufacturing from FDA-standard facilities. The Sentry multivitamin at 6 cents a serving beats Nature Made by 50% and NOW Foods by 35%.

**Generic forms over patented ingredients.** 21st Century uses magnesium oxide instead of Albion TRAACS, ferrous sulfate instead of ferrous bisglycinate, and generic lactobacillus blends instead of specific named strains. That keeps costs down but means the formulas rely on the most basic supplement science rather than patented ingredients.

**cGMP manufacturing.** The company operates FDA-inspected facilities and tests every batch. It does not hold NSF or USP certification on end products, but it does test for potency and purity internally.

## 4. Awards and certifications

21st Century does not seek third-party awards or end-product certifications. The company holds:

- cGMP manufacturing designation (FDA-inspected facilities)
- Non-GMO and gluten-free formula declarations (label-level, not Non-GMO Project verified)
- Halal certification on selected products (Women's Multi, Healthy Eyes)

There are no independently issued product awards in the brand's history. All quality claims are internal (batch testing, FDA facility inspection) rather than third-party audited.

## 5. Where the brand is weaker

The budget formula means trade-offs. Magnesium oxide is cheaper but less absorbed than chelates. Ferrous sulfate can constipate. Generic probiotic strains mean you cannot match a product to specific clinical conditions. Most importantly, there is no USP or NSF certification on end products — you are trusting 21st Century's internal testing rather than an independent audit. If you want third-party verification, Nature Made's USP line or NOW Foods' GMP line will cost more but provide that layer of confidence.

## 6. Buying notes

Both Amazon and iHerb carry the full 21st Century catalog. iHerb prices are generally lower, but Amazon's Subscribe & Save option can match or beat them on large bottles. The Sentry multivitamin on Amazon is also listed as B0007PLP0C, which we verified matches the exact 300-tablet bottle. Watch for iHerb's 20–30% sitewide coupons to get prices even lower.

Many 21st Century products come in two sizes (60 and 100 count for multis, 100 and 300 for minerals). The larger bottle always wins on per-serving cost; if you are a consistent user, always compare both size prices before buying.
