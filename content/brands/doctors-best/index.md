Doctor's Best started in 1990 in California, founded by a supplement retailer and a practicing physician. The company's strategy was simple: build formulas around patented, branded ingredients rather than commodity extracts. Where a competitor would sell "magnesium," Doctor's Best sells "Albion TRAACS chelated magnesium." Where a competitor sells "coenzyme Q10," Doctor's Best pairs it with BioPerine black pepper. That strategy has remained consistent through two ownership changes — private equity in the early 2010s, then a 96.1% acquisition by Chinese supplement giant Kingdomway in 2015.

Today Doctor's Best carries roughly 120 SKUs, covering joint care, antioxidants, minerals and brain support. The brand has become the default choice for shoppers who want patented ingredients at accessible prices: the same patented curcumin or MSM from a specialty brand might cost 30% more per bottle.

## 1. What the brand actually is

Doctor's Best's identity rests on three things.

**Patented raw ingredients.** Albion TRAACS for minerals, OptiMSM for joint products, Kaneka Q+ and QH-Absorb for CoQ10, Curcumin C3 for turmeric, BioPerine for absorption, PepZin GI for gut support, Lutemax 2020 for eye care, NSK-SD for nattokinase. Every branded ingredient carries published clinical data, and Doctor's Best uses them at evidence-based doses rather than filling bottles with generic extracts.

**Mid-tier pricing for patented formulas.** Because the company sources at volume and sells through iHerb and Amazon, it can offer patented-formula products at prices that beat specialty brands. Doctor's Best High Absorption CoQ10 with BioPerine is roughly 25% cheaper than comparable BioPerine pairs from other manufacturers.

**Chinese ownership with US-based operations.** Kingdomway, Doctor's Best's majority owner, is a major supplement manufacturer in China. Doctor's Best itself operates from California, uses third-party lab testing and publishes batch data on its website.

Against NOW Foods, Doctor's Best wins on patented-formula credibility for specific categories (joint care, CoQ10, curcumin) but loses on catalog breadth. Against Sports Research, Doctor's Best is cheaper across the board but lacks the fish-oil purity certifications that make SR a top choice for omega-3s. Against California Gold Nutrition, Doctor's Best is pricier but uses more patented ingredients; CGN is budget-first but does use Albion and OptiMSM on some products too.

Doctor's Best certifications and quality programs:

- **Internal batch testing**: Company-wide; batch COAs available on request

No independently issued product awards (Vity Awards, Better Nutrition, etc.) have been verified in the brand's history.

## 5. Where the brand is weaker

Three limitations stand out. First, shellfish: the flagship Glucosamine Chondroitin MSM contains shellfish-derived glucosamine, so vegetarians and shellfish-allergic shoppers need alternatives (CGN's GreenGrown glucosamine is shellfish-free). Second, catalog breadth: at roughly 120 SKUs, Doctor's Best is narrower than NOW Foods (1,400+ SKUs) and lacks children's or pet lines. Third, no end-product USP or NSF certification: the brand tests internally and uses patented ingredients, but it does not seek independent product-level certification like Nature Made does for its USP line.

## 6. Buying notes

iHerb is the primary channel and typically the lowest price for Doctor's Best products. Amazon sells the same formulas, but pricing can swing between Amazon.com and third-party sellers on the buy box. The patented ingredient brands (Albion, OptiMSM, Curcumin C3, BioPerine, PepZin GI, Lutemax, NSK-SD) are the real reason to buy Doctor's Best — always compare per-ingredient dose and per-serving price against competitors that use the same patented ingredients.
