Garden of Life started in 2000 by a Jordan Rubin, who built the brand around one simple idea: supplements should be made from real food, not synthetic chemicals. Over 26 years the company has grown into the largest organic supplement brand in the US, with more than 400 SKUs across multivitamins, probiotics, collagen and specialty formulas. Today Garden of Life is owned by Nestlé Health Science (acquired 2017), but the organic/whole-food positioning remains unchanged.

The brand's flagship lines are Organics (certified organic whole-food multivitamins and specialty products) and Dr. Formulated (clinically designed probiotics and condition-specific supplements). Both lines share the same foundation: Non-GMO Project Verified, USDA Organic (where applicable), no synthetic colors, flavors or sweeteners, and vegan or vegetarian formulations wherever possible.

## 1. What the brand actually is

Garden of Life's identity rests on three things.

**USDA Organic + Non-GMO Project Verified formulas.** The Organics line and most Vitamin Code products carry both certifications simultaneously — a rare combination in the supplement industry that signals thorough farm-to-bottle traceability.

**Whole-food formulations.** Instead of starting with synthetic vitamins and minerals, Garden of Life extracts nutrients from real fruits, vegetables and algae. The Organics Women's Once Daily, for example, gets its vitamin A from carrots, vitamin D3 from lichen, and vitamin C from amla berry, all certified organic.

**Vegan-first positioning.** Unlike most supplement brands, Garden of Life makes vegan products the default. The Organics line is vegan certified, and the Dr. Formulated probiotics use plant-based capsules. Even the collagen line adds probiotics to support gut health alongside joint support.

## 4. Awards and certifications

Garden of Life holds multiple third-party certifications:

- **USDA Organic**: Organics line (multivitamins, calcium, turmeric), Raw Probiotics Ultimate Care
- **Non-GMO Project Verified**: Organics, Vitamin Code, Dr. Formulated most products
- **NSF Certified for Sport**: Grass Fed Collagen Peptides
- **B Corp**: Corporate-level certification (positive impact on society/environment)
- **Vegan, OU Kosher**: Selected SKUs

There are no independently issued product awards (Vity Awards, Better Nutrition, etc.) in the brand's verified history.

## 5. Where the brand is weaker

Three limitations. First, price premium is significant: Organics formulas cost 4–6x commodity rivals (Nature Made, CGN) and 2–3x Albion/Patented-ingredient rivals (Doctor's Best, Jarrow). You are paying for certification, not clinical superiority. Second, the "whole food" claim is aspirational rather than literal — most active ingredients in multivitamins are still synthetic, just mixed with organic vegetable powder. Third, the probiotic naming is vague for some formulations (Dr. Formulated "50B" does not list strain names on the bottle; you need to check the website for clinical strain data).

## 6. Buying notes

Both Amazon and iHerb carry the full Garden of Life catalog. The Organic certification is the real premium — always check the USDA Organic seal on Organics line products. If organic is not a hard requirement, the Dr. Formulated probiotics still carry Non-GMO Project Verified but cost slightly less than Organics specialty products. The Grass Fed Collagen Peptides with NSF certification is the standout value — same grass-fed sourcing as premium organic collagen but NSF adds banned-substance testing, an extra layer of confidence for athletes.

Do not assume the "Raw" line means raw in every sense — for multivitamins it means unheated, not truly raw fruits and vegetables. The biggest price premiums are on Organics line multivitamins; the biggest value is on Dr. Formulated probiotics (highest CFU per dollar among certified organic probiotics).
