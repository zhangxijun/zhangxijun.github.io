New Chapter started in 1982 in a Vermont kitchen, making herbal formulas for local
practitioners. Four decades later it is a Procter & Gamble subsidiary, but the catalog still
looks unlike a mass-market vitamin brand: fermented herbs, plant-based calcium from marine
algae, and a shorter list of SKUs than NOW or Nature's Bounty carry.

The company's own line is that it was the first to ferment vitamins and herbs before
encapsulating them. Whether or not fermentation improves absorption in humans is still
debated, but it is the reason the brand's multivitamins and turmeric blends sit at a higher
price than the same nutrients from 21st Century or Swanson.

## 1. What the brand actually is

New Chapter's identity rests on three things.

**Fermentation.** Herbs and vitamins are cultured with probiotics before drying, a process
the company says makes nutrients easier on the stomach. Zyflamend, its ten-herb turmeric
blend, is the best-known example.

**Plant-based calcium.** Bone Strength Take Care uses calcium from *Lithothamnion* red
marine algae harvested off Iceland, paired with vitamins D3 and K2. That is a different
starting material from the calcium carbonate in a drugstore tablet, and it is why the
product costs more per serving than Nature Made.

**Sourcing claims.** The company publishes the origin of many ingredients and holds
Non-GMO Project verification, NSF gluten-free certification and OU Kosher certification
across most of the line.

## 2. How it compares with other brands

| | New Chapter | Garden of Life | MegaFood | NOW Foods |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | Fermented herbs, plant calcium | Certified organic, whole-food | Food-paired vitamins | Widest catalog, low price |
| **Price tier** | High | High | High | Low to mid |
| **Distinct claim** | Fermentation, red algae calcium | Organic certification | Farm sourcing | SKU count and price |
| **Best fit** | Bone health and turmeric | Organic-first shoppers | Food-based multis | Everyday restocking |

Against Garden of Life, New Chapter is less focused on organic certification and more
focused on fermentation and bone health. Against MegaFood, it has fewer food-sourced
vitamins but a stronger joint and bone lineup. Against NOW, it costs roughly twice as much
for comparable nutrients, and the difference is process and sourcing rather than dose.

## 3. Popular products

The 16 products below cover the brand's main bone, herbal, multivitamin and specialty
formulas. They are grouped by job so that similar labels are easier to compare.

### 3.1 Bone and calcium

#### Bone Strength Take Care

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="1" >}}

The bottle that built the brand's reputation. Each serving is three tablets carrying 905 mg of
calcium from *Lithothamnion* red algae, plus 1,000 IU of D3, K1, K2 and 62 mg of magnesium.
That is a different raw material from the calcium carbonate in a drugstore tablet, which is
why it costs roughly ten times more per day than Nature Made's 600 mg product. What makes
people stop is the three-tablet serving, not the price; if large doses are a struggle, this is
the wrong bottle. It ranks among our [bone and joint picks](/blog/best-bone-joint-supplements/).

### 3.2 Joint and herbal blends

Zyflamend is the multi-herb formula. Turmeric Force and Cinnamon Force are the simpler
choices for shoppers who want one featured botanical instead of a ten-herb blend.

#### Zyflamend

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="2" >}}

Ten herbs in one 780 mg serving, with turmeric holding 110 mg of that total. Buy it when the
appeal is one bottle instead of four and your stomach tolerates berberine. Skip it if you are
chasing a clinical curcumin dose: the full bottle delivers less curcumin than a 15 dollar
single-herb extract. The per-herb math is in our [full review](/blog/zyflamend-joint-review/).

#### Turmeric Force

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="13" >}}

One capsule combines 320 mg of turmeric water extract with 80 mg of organic supercritical
extract, for 400 mg in total. It is the simpler choice for someone who wants New Chapter's
turmeric without the other nine herbs in Zyflamend. The label emphasizes whole turmeric
rather than isolated curcumin, so its 400 mg cannot be compared directly with a 95%
curcuminoid extract. At about 43 cents a day, it costs much less than Zyflamend, but the two
bottles are not equivalent formulas.

#### Cinnamon Force

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="16" >}}

Each capsule supplies 105 mg of cassia cinnamon and 35 mg of Ceylon cinnamon, split between
water/alcohol and supercritical extracts. That gives shoppers both cinnamon species in one
one-a-day bottle rather than a kitchen-spice dose or a multi-herb blend. The formula contains
140 mg of cinnamon extracts in total, and the 44 iHerb ratings are still a fairly small review
base. The card shows the sale price observed on November 21, 2026, not a permanent discount.

#### Every Woman's One Daily

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="7" >}}

The standard women's formula provides 90 mg of vitamin C, 1,000 IU of D3, 400 mcg DFE of
folate, 4.8 mcg of B12, 5.5 mg of zinc and 40 mcg of selenium in one tablet. Its 400 mcg DFE
of folate is twice the amount in the women's 40+ and 55+ versions, while those later-life
formulas use different B12 amounts and herbal blends. The 72-count bottle lasts 72 days and
costs about 53 cents a day.

#### Every Man's One Daily

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="8" >}}

This standard men's multi combines 1,000 IU of D3 with 11 mg of zinc, 60 mcg of selenium and
a 45 mg stress and energy herbal blend. Its zinc dose is higher than the 7.4 mg in Every
Man's 40+, while the 40+ version instead adds an 89.8 mg prostate blend and leaves out iron.
Choose between them by comparing those formula differences rather than assuming the age label
automatically makes one stronger.

#### Every Woman's One Daily 40+

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="3" >}}

One tablet a day, even on an empty stomach: 1,000 IU of fermented D3, 10 mcg of
methylcobalamin and 200 mcg DFE of folate as L-5-methylfolate. Iron is deliberately absent,
which suits women past menopause or anyone whose ferritin is already high. The trade-off is
mineral coverage. There is 8.4 mg of magnesium and no calcium, so bone support has to come
from another bottle.

#### Every Man's One Daily 40+

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="4" >}}

One tablet carrying 7.4 mg of zinc, 60 mcg of selenium and an 89.8 mg prostate blend of nettle
root, pumpkin seed oil and saw palmetto. Saw palmetto's trial record for urinary symptoms is
mixed, so treat that blend as a bonus rather than the reason to buy. No iron, which is the
right call for most men over 40.

#### Every Woman's One Daily 55+

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="9" >}}

This iron-free formula uses 200 mcg DFE of methylfolate and 10 mcg of methyl B12 alongside
1,000 IU of D3. It also includes 60 mg of organic *Haematococcus* algae and 42.5 mg of organic
turmeric. The 40 mg of calcium is a small addition, not enough to replace a dedicated calcium
supplement. At roughly 90 cents a day, it costs more than the standard and 40+ women's multis.

#### Every Man's One Daily 55+

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="10" >}}

One iron-free tablet provides 67.5 mg of vitamin C, 1,000 IU of D3, 10 mcg of B12, 5 mg of
zinc and 60 mcg of selenium. The extras are 60 mg of organic algae and a 25 mg
prostate-support blend. That blend is much smaller than a standalone prostate formula, so it
is best viewed as an add-on to the multi rather than a substitute for Prostate 5LX.

#### Perfect Prenatal

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="6" >}}

Three tablets a day carry 680 mcg DFE of methylfolate, 20 mg of iron fermented with
probiotics and 62 mg of ginger aimed at nausea. The D3 is vegan and sourced from pine, which
matters if you avoid lanolin. At about 86 cents a day it costs more than One Daily Prenatal
35+, and the three-tablet routine is less convenient; its distinguishing details are the
1,000 IU of D3, ginger and longer-established review base.

#### One Daily Prenatal 35+

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="11" >}}

This prenatal reduces the routine to one tablet with 680 mcg DFE of methylfolate, 20 mg of
iron, 800 IU of D3, 55 mg of choline and 5 mg of B6. A 65 mg digestive herbal blend is also
included. It costs about 71 cents a day and the 90-count bottle lasts three months. Against
Perfect Prenatal, the appeal is one tablet and a lower daily cost; the trade-off is 800 IU
rather than 1,000 IU of D3 and a different herbal profile.

### 3.4 Magnesium

#### Magnesium Complex

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="5" >}}

Two tablets deliver 325 mg of elemental magnesium from three sources: seawater, TRAACS
bisglycinate and fermented magnesium. That split is easier on the gut than magnesium oxide,
and 46 cents a serving is fair for a chelated blend. The weakness is evidence. Only nine iHerb
ratings exist so far, and NOW's glycinate still costs less per 300 mg.

### 3.5 Specialty formulas

These three bottles are more focused than the multivitamins: one beauty formula, one prostate
blend and one standalone vitamin D3 product.

#### Hair, Skin & Nails

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="12" >}}

Each vegan capsule supplies 400 mcg of fermented biotin and 4 mg of astaxanthin from 120 mg
of organic algae, plus 100 mg of organic reishi. The formula also contains a 56.2 mg stress
and energy blend and a 37.5 mg digestive blend. This is not a high-dose standalone biotin
product; its distinguishing ingredient is the astaxanthin alongside a moderate biotin dose.
One capsule a day puts the cost at roughly 75 cents.

#### Prostate 5LX

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="14" >}}

Two capsules provide 320 mg of saw palmetto, 100 mg of green tea, 96 mg of pumpkin seed oil,
75 mg of nettle root, 80 mg of ginger extracts and 50 mcg of selenium. It is a much more
focused formula than the small prostate blends in the men's multis, and the 180-capsule bottle
lasts 90 days. Saw palmetto's clinical record is mixed, so the dose and ingredient list are
more useful buying guides than promises of urinary relief.

#### Fermented Vitamin D3

{{< product-grid data="new-chapter" item="15" >}}

One tablet supplies 50 mcg (2,000 IU) of fermented D3 with 120 mg of organic turmeric and
20 mg of organic reishi. It suits someone who wants vitamin D without the rest of a
multivitamin. At about 50 cents a day it costs more than a basic D3 tablet; the premium pays
for New Chapter's fermentation process and companion herbs, not a higher vitamin D dose.

Third-party certifications:

- Non-GMO Project Verified
- NSF Certified Gluten Free
- OU Kosher
- Certified Organic by Where Food Comes From (Bone Strength Tiny Tabs)
- TESTED by SuppCo, an independent program that buys products anonymously and tests them,

Two claims on the packaging are the company's own: "#1 fermented multivitamin" and "#1
natural calcium brand." Neither is independently audited in the way a certification is, so
treat them as marketing rather than a measured market share.

## 5. Where the brand is weaker

The premium is real: per serving, New Chapter usually costs more than NOW, Doctor's Best or
21st Century for the same nutrient. Fermentation has limited published human data behind
it, so you are paying partly for a process whose benefit is plausible rather than proven.
Bone Strength Take Care also needs three tablets per serving, which is a hard sell for
anyone who dislikes large doses.

## 6. Buying notes

Both Amazon and iHerb carry the full line, and the price gap between them swings by
product. Check the tablet count before comparing: Bone Strength Take Care ships in 60, 120
and 180-count bottles, and the per-serving cost changes with the size. Zyflamend is sold in
a single 180-capsule bottle.
